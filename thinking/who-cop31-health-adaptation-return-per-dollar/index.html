WHO says USD 24 back per dollar. Health adaptation still gets a third of its bill.

Abhijith Magal · 3 November 2026 · 8 min read

TL;DR

  • WHO's briefing for COP31 in Antalya says six prioritised climate-health actions, scaled up, could save 1.6 million lives a year and return around USD 24 for every dollar invested. It announces an Antalya Initiative for Health Resilience to move the Belem Health Action Plan from commitment to implementation. Health Day is 9 November 2026.
  • The Lancet Countdown 2026 puts the other side of the ledger: 71 per cent of WHO member states had no plan to adapt their health systems by 2025, developing countries costed health adaptation at USD 3.3 billion a year, and donors committed USD 1.1 billion in 2024.
  • An ADB working paper from 15 October finds less than 1 per cent of international adaptation finance is aimed primarily at health, and private money is 3 per cent of adaptation finance in poorer countries.
  • India has a national plan and is on the right side of the 71 per cent. What nobody has shown a funder is what the USD 24 return looks like in one district, at one site, for one roster. That is a proof a CSR committee can pay for, and the Antalya vocabulary will be the one head office uses from December.

For the first time this cycle, WHO has put a price and a return on climate-health action. Its briefing for COP31, dated 3 November 2026, says 3.3 to 3.6 billion people live in settings highly vulnerable to climate change, that scaling up six prioritised actions could save 1.6 million lives a year, and that each dollar invested would return around USD 24. It asks for four things: cut emissions, adapt with health in mind, finance what works, and put health in the negotiated text. And it announces an Antalya Initiative for Health Resilience, to work with the Alliance for Transformative Action on Climate and Health in moving the Belem Health Action Plan from commitment to implementation. Health Day in Antalya is 9 November.

A return figure is the language a CSR committee understands, which is why it matters more than the lives figure. It also arrives beside a set of numbers that explain why the return has not been collected.

What does the money side actually look like?

Three documents, read together, give the shape. The Lancet Countdown's tenth report, launched on 28 October, counts 710,000 heat-related deaths in 2023, 71.5 per cent of them attributable to climate change, and 573 billion potential work hours lost to heat in 2025, worth USD 1.07 trillion. On adaptation it finds 71 per cent of member states without a health-system plan, climate-informed health early warning in 75 countries, and a target of every country by the end of 2027. Developing countries costed their health adaptation needs at USD 3.3 billion a year; donors committed USD 1.1 billion in 2024. Aid from the 17 largest donors is on course to fall by more than USD 50 billion between 2023 and 2026.

The Asian Development Bank's working paper of 15 October, written with Health Care Without Harm, gives the instrument-level view. Less than 5 per cent of international adaptation finance includes health as a component, less than 1 per cent is aimed primarily at health, private finance is 3 per cent of adaptation finance in low- and middle-income countries, and more than 90 per cent of ADB and IDB climate-health flows have been loans. Its India case study is the one I keep coming back to: SEWA's heat insurance for informal women workers pays out when a temperature trigger is crossed, and "continues to rely on philanthropic funding to support insurance premiums". I wrote about the design in heat insurance pays the worker. The delivery mechanism works. The payer is missing.

And WHO's own release of 28 October, answering the Countdown within hours, says climate-driven health events may meet the criteria for a public health emergency of international concern under the International Health Regulations, and that it is already running a graded emergency response for the current El Nino. That is the legal register in which COP31's health conversation will now be held.

Where does India sit in this?

On the right side of the 71 per cent, and that is where the comfort ends. India has a national programme on climate change and human health and most states carry heat action plans, so the question an Indian funder faces is not whether a plan exists. It is whether the plan reaches anyone on a roster. The Lancet Countdown's 2026 India data sheet, read in six numbers in operating terms, carries no adaptation indicator at all. In the same fortnight, Andhra Pradesh took two cyclones and published careful pre-landfall health inventories with no count of anyone on daily treatment, covered in two cyclones, one register. The plan exists. The patient-level number does not.

That is the gap WHO's USD 24 cannot fill from Geneva. A return figure is a global average of modelled actions. What a CSR committee in Mumbai needs, and what no document in this fortnight provides, is what the return looks like at one site in one district over one season. Which workers were kept on shift, which children stayed in school, which patients stayed on treatment, and what it cost.

What a company does with these numbers before 9 November

Expect the vocabulary. From December, a head-office sustainability team that attended Antalya will ask its India arm what it is doing on health resilience, in the Antalya Initiative's own terms, and I set out in head office has a climate-health mandate why an answer made of activity, with no exposure read behind it, fails. Have the exposure read ready before the question arrives.

Then fund the proof, not the pledge. Section 135 money can go to a programme that measures what heat does at a specific site and changes it. Not a declaration, not a camp, a measured season. The private share of adaptation finance in poorer countries is 3 per cent, which means a single Indian company that funds one district-level climate-health programme with a counted outcome is, in that market, unusual enough to be quoted. The line item is defensible under Schedule VII, and the measurement maps to BRSR.

The global return is priced. The district-level proof is not. One company can fund the second, and the first will be quoted back to it anyway.

I measure what heat does to the people a company depends on, at the sites where they work and learn, and I build the programme the company pays for to change that number. The two offers that do it are a Heat-Safe Workers season at a company's own sites and a Heat-Ready Schools sprint at the schools its workforce depends on. Each produces the number WHO's briefing assumes and no Indian document yet holds: a counted outcome, at a site, through a season, with the cost beside it. That is what USD 24 per dollar looks like at a scale a CSR committee can sign.

Definition · climate-access

Climate-access is the gap between healthcare that exists on paper and healthcare people can actually reach once climate disruption is counted, read across four dimensions: availability, affordability, reachability and continuity. A return figure that is never measured at a site is a plan on paper.

The caveats a careful reader will want

WHO's page carried a 3 November date before Health Day and does not list the six actions in its summary, so the return figure should be quoted as WHO's pre-COP position until the full briefing is read. The 0.5 per cent share of climate finance for health that circulates alongside these numbers was questioned by Germany's climate ministry in mid-October, so the figure itself will be argued over at Antalya. And the ADB figures on adaptation finance cite a 2023 analysis. None of that moves the operating conclusion, which rests on what a company can measure at its own sites, not on which global percentage survives the negotiation.

Where this is headed

Antalya will produce an initiative with a name, a pavilion from 9 to 20 November and a set of asks that every health ministry and funder will quote for a year. The Belem plan's own word is now "implementation". The companies that can say, in December, what they implemented at which site with which result will own that conversation in India. The rest will be asked, in Antalya's vocabulary, why they cannot.
