From Belém to Antalya: what the first health adaptation plan means for companies in India.

Abhijith Magal · 21 September 2026 · 8 min read

TL;DR

  • The Belém Health Action Plan, launched at COP30 on 13 November 2025, is the first international climate adaptation plan written for health. It is voluntary and aimed at governments.
  • By May 2026, 33 countries had endorsed it. We found no public record of India among them.
  • It binds no company. It reaches an Indian subsidiary indirectly, through a parent headquartered in an endorsing country and through the supply-chain and surveillance work it asks health systems to do.
  • Two of its three action lines give a company's access programme somewhere real to plug in. The third mostly does not. COP31 opens with a Health Day on 9 November.

The Belém Health Action Plan India question is a strange one to be asking, because India does not appear to be in it. The plan was launched by Brazil's COP30 Presidency on Health Day, 13 November 2025. WHO reported in May 2026 that 33 countries had endorsed it and 50 organisations supported it. As of this week, I could not find India on any published list.

That gap is the reason a company operating in India should read it. A subsidiary whose parent sits in Tokyo, Paris, Amsterdam, Oslo or Madrid now reports to a head office whose government has signed a health adaptation plan. The Indian arm operates in a country that has not. Language travels down a corporate structure faster than it travels across a treaty, and the question from head office tends to arrive before anyone in India has read the document.

What the plan says

The full title is the Belém Health Action Plan for the Adaptation of the Health Sector to Climate Change. It runs to 23 pages, and it is built around three lines of action with two principles over all of them. The principles are health equity and climate justice, and leadership and governance with social participation. The lines are these.

The plan also has a history worth knowing. WHO's Global Action Plan on Climate Change and Health was approved by the World Health Assembly in May 2025 under decision WHA78(27). The Belém plan is the narrower instrument: it takes the adaptation priorities of that broader plan and turns them into actionable items under each line. Anyone at head office citing one is likely to cite the other.

Why it reaches an Indian subsidiary at all

Nothing in the plan applies to a company. It seeks voluntary endorsement from UNFCCC Parties, and invites statements of support from international organisations, civil society and non-state actors. That is the whole of the private sector's formal role.

The route in is organisational, not legal. Brazil's official endorsement list includes France, Spain, Portugal, Japan, Norway, Canada and the United Kingdom, and Australia, joint holder of the incoming COP31 Presidency, endorsed in November 2025. A parent company headquartered in one of those countries meets health adaptation as national policy at home. When it asks its subsidiaries what they are doing, the Indian arm needs an answer that is specific and measurable, and "we fund a health camp" will not survive the second question.

The plan binds no company. The question from head office will still arrive, and it will arrive in the plan's vocabulary.

That is the HQ-mandate budget in its cleanest form: a reporting expectation set from outside India, landing on a team inside it. The disclosure side of the same pressure, where India's own regulator already asks for numbers, is set out in the BRSR disclosures most companies under-report.

Who owns what

Figure 2 · Government, company, partner

A company that claims a government function under the plan's name is making a claim it cannot evidence. The data zone is where it can contribute without overreaching.

The useful discipline is to keep the three columns apart. Surveillance, policy and public facilities belong to government, and a company programme should not describe itself as doing any of them. The company owns its workforce, its product's route to market and the programmes it pays for. The implementing partner owns what happens in the field, including whether delivery holds in the weeks when conditions turn, which is the question at the centre of the partner screening checklist.

Where the three meet is data. The first action line asks for surveillance that sees vulnerable populations. A funded programme already collects a weekly record of who it reached. If that record is kept by week, overlaid on heat and flood days, and shared in a form a district officer can read, the programme has contributed to the first action line without pretending to run it.

Before Antalya

COP31 runs from 9 to 20 November 2026 in Antalya, under a Presidency shared by Türkiye and Australia. Resilient health systems are among the Presidency's ten priority themes. Health Day opens the conference on 9 November, followed by a day for ATACH, the WHO-led climate and health alliance, on 10 November.

Three things are worth doing before then. Find out whether your parent's home government has endorsed the plan, since that decides whether the question is coming. Map your existing access or CSR health work against the three lines in the table above, including the cells that stay empty. And pick one programme where a weekly, weather-overlaid record is feasible this year. That record is the most credible thing an Indian subsidiary can put in front of head office, and it is the same record climate-access asks for.

Common questions

What is the Belém Health Action Plan?

The Belém Health Action Plan for the Adaptation of the Health Sector to Climate Change was launched by Brazil's COP30 Presidency on 13 November 2025, the first Health Day at a climate COP. It sets out three lines of action for climate-resilient health systems: surveillance and monitoring; evidence-based policies, strategies and capacity-building; and innovation, production and digital health. Two cross-cutting principles sit over all three: health equity and climate justice, and leadership and governance with social participation.

Does the Belém Health Action Plan apply to companies?

No. It is voluntary and written for governments, which endorse it. It invites statements of support from non-state actors, including the private sector, but places no obligation on any company. Its effect on companies is indirect: through the governments and parent companies that adopt its language, and through the supply-chain and surveillance work it asks health systems to do.

Has India endorsed the Belém Health Action Plan?

As of 21 September 2026, Syntropy Earth could find no public record of India endorsing the plan. By May 2026 it had been endorsed by 33 countries and supported by 50 organisations, according to WHO. Check the current list before relying on this answer, since endorsements continue.

What will happen on health at COP31?

COP31 runs from 9 to 20 November 2026 in Antalya, Türkiye. Resilient health systems are one of the COP31 Presidency's ten priority themes, and the conference opens with a dedicated Health Day on 9 November, followed by a day for ATACH, the WHO-led climate and health alliance, on 10 November.

How does the Belém plan relate to WHO's Global Action Plan on climate change and health?

WHO's Global Action Plan on Climate Change and Health was approved by the World Health Assembly in May 2025 under decision WHA78(27) and covers all climate and health priorities. The Belém plan is narrower. It takes the adaptation priorities of the Global Action Plan and turns them into actionable items under each line.
