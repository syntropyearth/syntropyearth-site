The flagship

Social Responsibility Programme Design

Programme design · board-ready output

A social responsibility programme built around your own mission, designed to survive a board question, an auditor, and a change of CSR head.

Most companies arrive believing they need a new programme. Usually they need the one they already run to be counted properly. The work starts by reading your existing portfolio and your own reporting, because in nearly every case something valuable is being undercounted or tagged against the wrong goal. A veterinary camp that quietly registers families for health cover gets filed under land and biodiversity, and the health access it created disappears from the record.

From there: a theory of change tied to the core business rather than a cause list, selection criteria and a screening scorecard for implementing partners, a measurement approach mapped to BRSR and GRI so it reports without rework, a three-year roadmap with milestone gates, and the board presentation to carry it.

Scope modules, chosen by your industry

  • Supply chain and cold chain climate resilience
  • Facility and infrastructure resilience
  • Workforce heat-health protocol for distributed or field teams
  • Community access mapping

One thing worth knowing before we talk. Expenses directly incurred for designing, monitoring and evaluating a specific project count as project cost, not against your administrative overhead cap. Rule 2(1)(b) and MCA General Circular 14/2021 say so plainly. Most CSR heads have never been told.

For pharma, healthcare and diagnostics

Health Equity Programme Design

Programme design · ready to fund and launch

A patient access or health equity programme where climate vulnerability sits inside the allocation logic from the first version, rather than being discovered after launch.

Most health equity programmes find their climate blind spot once the money is already committed to delivery, when fixing it is expensive and visible to everyone. This puts the fix in first.

Access-barrier and disease-burden mapping at district level rather than state level, disaggregated by climate vulnerability so allocation weights toward the geographies carrying the highest load. The patient pathway designed across diagnosis, initiation, adherence and continuity, with seasonal disruption modelled at every step, because a programme that holds in November can fail in May. Cold chain and last-mile continuity wherever medicines move. Governance that keeps the programme cleanly separated from promotional activity. Seasonal measurement built into the indicator set from the beginning, not retrofitted when a funder asks.

This one is for pharmaceutical, healthcare and diagnostics organisations. If your programme sits outside that, the social responsibility design above is the right shape and we will say so on the first call.

The diagnostic

Climate Access Stress Test

Scored · evidenced · disclosure-ready

CAST scores a health or access programme against a full climate year, so you know which break to fix first and can prove it to an auditor.

Four dimensions, four seasons, one score. Geographic exposure. Supply chain and operational continuity. Workforce availability. Financial and demand continuity. Each read across heat, monsoon, air and drought, because a single annual number hides the month the programme actually fails.

The stage that changes minds is pathway analysis: showing where one break cascades into three others. A flooded road is not a road problem. It is a missed follow-up, a broken cold chain, a field team that cannot travel, and a demand curve that moves without warning.

What you hold at the end

  • A scored vulnerability assessment across all four dimensions
  • Defensible climate-access metrics that lift straight into a BRSR or funder report
  • A ranked set of interventions with named owners and indicative costs
  • A board-ready readout

The calendar is unhurried on purpose. You need room to consult internally between stages, and a rushed diagnostic produces a document nobody acts on. CAST runs inside a design engagement or on its own.

Design to running programme

Implementation and Handover

Build, train, hand over

The operating machine that runs the designed programme, handed to a named person on your side who has been trained to run it.

A strategy in a drawer helps nobody, and neither does a permanent dependence on an outside adviser. This closes that gap. The partner screening scorecard applied to a real shortlist rather than handed over blank. An onboarding kit covering due diligence, MCA registration checks, agreements and reporting obligations. A governance and escalation model that names who decides what. A monitoring system with an indicator set, a collection rhythm and a reporting template. A disbursement and compliance calendar aligned to the financial year. Then a training block with the person who will own it, and a written operating manual they keep.

Two routes open at the end, and both are put on the table in writing. Run it yourself with your trained project manager. Or keep us on a retainer for partner oversight. Neither is presented as the better deal, because the right answer depends on your capacity, not on our margin.

Where it becomes ongoing

The recurring engagement

Advisory Retainer

Monthly · quarterly renewal

Standing oversight of a live programme, and someone who already knows your operation when the next decision lands.

A monthly strategic brief of a page or two on the last working day. A running decision log, so a change of personnel does not erase the reasoning behind a choice made in March. A watchlist of exposure that is starting to move. Implementing-partner review. A quarterly review pack at each renewal point.

Tiers move on named scope drivers, the number of partners, the number of sites, the reporting rhythm, so a change is a scope conversation rather than a negotiation about worth. This almost always follows a design engagement, because the design is what proves the value.

  • An access-readiness checklist

The wider practice

Climate Literacy & Speaking

Build climate fluency inside a team using your own operations as the working material, not a general climate primer.

Either a half-day leadership briefing, or a four-session working series with an exercise in every session, so the group produces something rather than sits through something. Sessions are built on your own exposure, which is why they reliably surface programme gaps nobody had named yet.

Also guest lectures and teaching modules for schools, colleges and universities, capped at six a year and tracked, because the cap is what keeps them good. And keynotes for industry events, scoped to the room rather than delivered from stock.

Launch Readiness Advisory

Read a launch plan against the four climate-access dimensions while the plan can still change.

Every launch plan carries assumptions about conditions. Distribution reach that holds through monsoon months. A field force available through peak heat. Cold chain integrity across the whole route. Demand seasonality that moves with weather rather than with the financial calendar. When one of those is wrong, it is usually found after the budget is committed.

The output is a climate-adjusted plan with named risks, mitigations, and the decision points where each has to be settled. Two shapes: a full read for a corporate market or product launch, and a compressed single-pass version for a venture entering a new geography, delivered as a working document with the founder in the room.

This stays anchored in climate-access. General brand and go-to-market consulting is not on offer here. Any good brand agency does that work, does it well, and does it cheaper.

Institutional Programme Work

Programme structuring, research support, evidence synthesis and delivery model design for UN bodies, development finance institutions and foundations.

The work sits inside the institution's own goals rather than alongside them, and is scoped and contracted the way institutional procurement actually tenders and evaluates. Procurement cycles here run six to eighteen months, so these relationships get seeded well before an RFP appears. If you are inside one of these organisations and something is forming, the earliest conversation is the useful one.

Most organisations start with a scorecard or a design engagement, add implementation once the programme is real, and continue on a retainer. Literacy and launch readiness widen from there. Institutional work is scoped by relationship.

If none of these is quite it

Organisations at different stages of growth carry different obligations, and some need something that sits outside every shape above. Saying that openly is more useful than bending a standard scope until it stops fitting.

One conversation to work out what is actually moving and why, then a scoped proposal with a fixed fee, a named deliverable set, a timeline and payment milestones, inside a week. The one thing we need before that call is worth naming: a budget the work attaches to. Without one it is a conversation we are glad to have, and we will be honest that it is not yet an engagement.

Where we refer out

Technical ESG, net-zero roadmapping, emissions accounting and disclosure reporting stay off the offer. They are specialist territory, and naming the edge keeps the work focused and the standard high. When you need that work, we point you to people who do it well. The same test applies to everything above: if you can do it in-house or buy it off a shelf, we will tell you so rather than sell you a version of it.
