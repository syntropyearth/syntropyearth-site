You measure the trees.
Nobody is measuring the health dividend.

A three-layer proposal, built around what SankalpTaru already has on the ground and in its data.

SankalpTaru already has the parts most organisations spend years assembling. Geotagging, IoTree, blockchain records, AI species selection, drone survey, satellite monitoring, survival rates per site, beneficiary counts across 28 states, and 2,800 corporate partners who have already paid for something.

What is not being counted anywhere is the human health return on all of it. PoshanKit is a nutrition intervention. The Mahindra award was for clean air. Pune is justified on heat island and pollution, Punjab on stubble burning and groundwater, Assam on flood recovery. The health argument sits inside every programme description and is measured in none of them.

Why this matters commercially

Your partners are the companies now heading into assured BRSR disclosure. They need social outcomes, not only environmental ones, and they need them from spend they have already committed. A plantation that can also report a health dividend answers two disclosure obligations from one budget line. That is a reason to renew, and a reason to increase.

What I am proposing

Three layers, in order

Layer one Start here

A free scorecard SankalpTaru gives to its corporate partners

Same mechanic as the one I have already built and put live. Free, self-serve, no sign-up, around ninety seconds, scored result on screen and by email. It asks a CSR or sustainability lead a short set of questions and tells them how defensible their current green spend is against the disclosure regime they are now inside.

Co-branded, hosted by SankalpTaru. It goes out cold to the 2,800 partner contacts already in your database as a genuine free test, not a pitch. Everyone who completes it hands you a live signal on where their programme is weak, which is the opening for a conversation about a bigger commitment.

You can see the working version of mine here: syntropyearth.com/climate-access

Layer two The substance underneath

The Plantation Health Dividend

This is what makes layer one unlike anything else being offered to your partners. A site-level score across five dimensions, all of which your existing data can already feed.

  • HeatCooling effect measured against the district's heat exposure and its outdoor working population.
  • AirParticulate reduction against measured local air quality. This is the clean air work you have already been recognised for, given a number.
  • NutritionFruit yield converted into dietary contribution for beneficiary households. PoshanKit stops being a pilot and becomes a reportable metric.
  • WaterGroundwater recharge and moisture retention set against district water stress.
  • Livelihood and healthIncome diversification as a determinant of care-seeking behaviour and household health spending.

The output turns "we planted 107,362 trees across Karnataka" into a defensible statement about what those trees did for the people living beside them. Same programme, same spend, a second outcome your partners can report.

Layer three Later, only if one and two land

Site selection guided by climate-health exposure

Choosing new programme districts by where climate is already interrupting health and access, rather than by where land happens to be available. It makes the siting decision itself evidence-led, and it gives a partner a reason to fund a specific district rather than a general number of trees.

This is a longer conversation and I would not open with it. It sits here so the direction is visible.
