Heat-Safe Workers season · workplace heat stress, measured

Heat is already costing your site. Now it has a number.

The Heat-Safe Workers season measures heat stress where your people work, for one full summer. You get the exposure record, the actions your site took, the health picture at the start and the end, and the days it cost you. Then you get the protocol that changes next year's number.

Who this is for

Any site where people work in the heat and someone answers for them.

  • Construction sites: metro, road, airport, real estate, and the contractors who run them
  • Plants, foundries, kilns, warehouses and yards where the heat comes from the process and the roof
  • Campuses and tech parks with housekeeping, security and landscaping teams outdoors all day
  • Field teams: delivery, sales, utilities, solar and mining
  • CSR committees funding a cohort that has no employer behind it
The problem, plainly

A hot summer leaves no invoice. It still gets paid.

It gets paid in slower afternoons and missed shifts. In a worker who goes home dizzy and does not come back for three days. In an alert-day order from the labour office that nobody on site can prove was followed. In one case that reaches a hospital and becomes an incident report.

None of it is recorded as heat, so none of it can be managed as heat. The weather forecast gives the city's temperature. Your people work on a slab in full sun, where the reading is a different one.

Most of what a heat-safe site needs is already owed under the construction and factory rules, and half of it is already in place: water, a rest shed, first aid. The season adds the missing piece, the measurement, and ties the rest to it.

  1. 01 / 08

    The site as it is today

    Your site already has a safety plan, a rest area and drinking water. What it does not have is a reading. On a hot afternoon nobody can say how hot the slab was, who was on it, or what it did to them.

    What goes on recordNothing yet. That is the starting point for almost every site in India.
  2. 02 / 08

    We walk the site with your safety officer

    One morning on site. We find the most exposed work zone, the rest area, and the corners where heat pools: the slab, the scaffold, the open yard. Instrument positions are agreed on the spot.

    What goes on recordA site code, a sketch of zones, and where each instrument goes.
  3. 03 / 08

    Two instruments and a shaded reference go up

    A wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) point at the hottest work zone. A second at the rest area. A shaded logger for air temperature and humidity. Each sits at 1.1 metres on a tripod or bracket and reads every 15 minutes, the way the Indian standard IS 16559 expects.

    What goes on recordThe exposure series: a reading every 15 minutes for the whole season.
  4. 04 / 08

    The band card goes on the wall

    A laminated card at each rest shed shows today's band: green, yellow, orange or red. Beside it, the action for that band. Your supervisor updates it twice a day from the morning and midday readings. Water and electrolytes sit at every work zone, and what is issued gets counted.

    What goes on recordThe action log: breaks taken, water and ORS issued, shifts moved.
  5. 05 / 08

    A red-band day arrives

    Nothing slows on a green day. On a red one, the site knows what to do before the heat does its damage. Heavy outdoor work stops in the peak hours. Slab and roof work moves to the morning. The cooling station is staffed and every reading is posted.

    What goes on recordStop hours, worker-days shifted, and whether the site did what the state asked that day.
  6. 06 / 08

    Screening at the start and the end

    A health camp in the baseline fortnight and another when the season closes: blood pressure, blood sugar, a kidney-function line where the doctor agrees, and a short heat-symptom questionnaire. In between, a field doctor reviews every case and decides referrals.

    What goes on recordThe health register: cases by grade, referrals, and the change between the two camps.
  7. 07 / 08

    Every Friday, one page

    The week's curve against the bands. Hours in each band. Actions taken. Cases. One recommendation. It goes on the rest-shed wall and into your safety head's inbox, so the season is managed while it is happening.

    What goes on recordA weekly readout, and a two-page note each month for the project director or CSR committee.
  8. 08 / 08

    Three weeks after the season, the evidence pack

    The Site Heat Report, your site's heat-safe protocol, the reporting paragraph, and your entry in the register. The season becomes something you can hand to a board, an assurer or a client.

    What goes on recordThe outcome counts: days lost per 1,000 worker-days, cases caught early, and next year's baseline.

The band card

Four bands. One card. Everyone on site knows what today asks of them.

The limits come from the international standard for heat stress, the same one India has adopted. The season puts a reading behind them, so your site does what its state's heat action plan already expects, and can show it.

Greenbelow 26 °C

Normal work. Water at every zone. The card is updated and the hours are counted.

Yellow26 to 28 °C

Water prompted every 20 minutes. A ten-minute shade break each hour for heavy work. The first-aider is briefed.

Orange28 to 31 °C

Heavy outdoor work limited. Fifteen minutes of rest in every hour. Roof and slab work moves to cooler hours. Buddy checks begin.

Redabove 31 °C

Heavy outdoor work stops in the peak hours. The cooling station is staffed. Any case is cooled on site first and transported second.

The card reads in wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT). Its four cut points sit inside the international reference range of ISO 7243:2017, adopted in India as IS 16559:2019, and match the WBGT table published in the Karnataka Heat Action Plan 2026. The colours are the four alert colours the India Meteorological Department uses nationwide.
Find your bands

Three rules meet at your site. See all three.

The world sets a limit for the body. India sets an alert for the district. Your state writes a plan. Choose where you are and what the work is.

1 · The global reference, for the body

28 °C WBGT

The reference limit for moderate work by a person used to the heat. For someone new to it, the limit is 26 °C. Above the limit, work needs rest breaks or a detailed assessment.

ISO 7243:2017, Annex A; in India, IS 16559:2019
2 · The national alert, for the district

40 °C air temperature

In the plains a heat wave is declared when the day's maximum reaches 40 °C and runs 4.5 °C or more above normal, or touches 45 °C outright. Yellow means be updated, orange be prepared, red take action.

India Meteorological Department, heat wave criteria and colour codes
3 · Your state's plan

Choose a state

Twenty-three heat-prone states work with the national disaster authority and the weather department on heat action plans. Pick yours to see where it stands.

Ministry of Earth Sciences, Lok Sabha reply, 26 July 2023

Rules 2 and 3 read the air over a district. Rule 1 reads the load on a body, and needs an instrument where that body is. A measured season puts that instrument on your site and writes all three onto one card.

    Twelve to fifteen pages. Exposure by hour and by zone, days in each band, actions taken, cases by grade, outcomes against your own baseline, and three recommendations for next season.

  2. Heat-safe protocol, version one, for your site

    The band card, rest and water rule, alert-day actions and referral path, written for your site's work and signed by your site head.

    Maps toBRSR Principle 3; GRI 403-1 and 403-2; your ISO 45001 hazard register

  3. The BRSR paragraph

    One paragraph on worker well-being and safety with the season's counted numbers, ready for your sustainability team to place.

    Maps toBRSR Principle 3, employees and workers including contract workers

  4. GRI 403 table

    One page that sets each season number against the occupational health and safety disclosures.

    Maps toGRI 403-3, 403-6, 403-9 and 403-10

  5. Outcomes for the people on site

    Cases caught early, referrals completed, days lost per 1,000 worker-days, by cohort. The beneficiary numbers a CSR report needs, counted from the register.

    Maps toSDG 3.d and SDG 8.8; Schedule VII item (i), preventive health care

  6. Your entry in the register

    The anonymised site-season, with your consent terms applied, and a benchmark against other sites once there are enough to compare.

Heat stress at work: questions a safety head asks first

Straight answers before the call.

What is a Heat-Safe Workers season?

It is one full heat season measured at your site. Instruments record wet-bulb globe temperature every 15 minutes at the point of work and the point of rest. Supervisors run a four-band card with set actions. A doctor screens workers at the start and the end. Three weeks after the season you receive a Site Heat Report, a heat-safe protocol and a reporting paragraph.

How do you measure heat stress at a worksite?

With wet-bulb globe temperature, or WBGT. It combines air temperature, humidity, radiant heat from the sun and hot surfaces, and air movement into one reading, which is why it tracks what a working body feels far better than a weather forecast does. The method follows IS 16559:2019, the Indian standard identical to ISO 7243:2017.

Does work have to stop on hot days?

Only heavy outdoor work, only in the red band, and only in the peak hours. Heat action plans already advise moving outdoor work out of the afternoon peak on alert days. The season gives your site the reading that tells you when the rule applies, moves heavy tasks to cooler hours, and records that you did it.

Who is covered: our employees or contract workers too?

Everyone on the site roster: employees, contract workers and migrant workers. The register holds codes and cohorts, never names, in our copy. Your site keeps its own full record.

What does the season ask of our team?

A safety officer who owns the protocol, shift supervisors who update the card twice a day and log actions on a phone form, and a wall for the card. Supervisor training takes 45 minutes. We place the instruments, run the readouts and write the pack.

How does it show up in our BRSR and GRI reporting?

The evidence pack includes one paragraph written for BRSR Principle 3 on the well-being of employees and workers, and a one-page table that maps each season number to GRI 403 disclosures on occupational health services, worker health promotion, injuries and ill health. For a CSR-funded season it also names the Schedule VII item. The pack says maps to, never satisfies: your assurer makes that call.

What does a season cost?

A fixed figure agreed in writing before anything starts, set by the number of sites and the number of people on them. Nothing is priced on this page because nothing is priced before we have seen the site.

We are outside Karnataka. Can we still do this?

Yes. Site walks in person happen in Karnataka. Everywhere else starts with a 20-minute call and a few photographs of the site, and the walk follows once a season is agreed. The band card is written to your own state's heat rules.
