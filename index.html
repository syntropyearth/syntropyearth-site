The Practices

Two practices. One standard of work.

Social Impact turns a committed budget into a programme that reports cleanly and survives scrutiny. Brand and Growth builds the commercial plan behind a launch, a portfolio or a market entry. Organisations engage one and frequently find the second answers a problem they had filed somewhere else. Both are scoped as defined pieces of work, with a fixed figure and a named deliverable agreed before anything begins.

Practice One

Social Impact

CSR and health equity programme design, then delivery. The work starts by reading what you already run, because most organisations are further along than their reporting suggests, and builds the programme that turns committed spend into a defensible outcome. Design is a defined piece of work with its own fee. Implementation follows on a retainer, or your team runs it and the handover is built for that.

You might be starting here

The spend is committed and the board wants to know what it bought. Section 135 money moving against activity counts that will not survive a serious question.
Head office set the target. You have to make it real here. A group commitment with a reporting date, written somewhere that has never seen your operating reality.
Your people are working through heat nobody has measured. Absence, attrition and incident cost carrying a signal that sits outside conventional safety systems.
The assurance window is opening. BRSR Core reasonable assurance, where activity counts are not outcomes and an assurer will say so.

How We Design

A programme is built, not commissioned.

This is the part most decks skip. The sequence below is what happens between a budget being approved and an outcome being reportable, and every step produces something you keep.

01

Mapping

Ethnographic work with the community the programme is meant to serve, plus a read of what you already run.

02

System design

The forces sitting behind the beneficiary, and where an intervention changes the system rather than the symptom.

03

Programme creation

Theory of change, partner architecture, fund flow, and the measurement that has to exist before anything runs.

04

Pilot

A bounded first run with the instrumentation already in place, so what you learn is evidence rather than anecdote.

05

Scaling

Extension to further sites or cohorts, with community ownership designed in rather than hoped for.

06

Impact documentation

The outcome written up against BRSR, GRI and the relevant SDGs, in a form an assurer accepts.

Who We Work With

The work spans industries, because the obligations do.

CSR and sustainability heads

Across steel, cement, mining, chemicals, FMCG, manufacturing, construction and agriculture, holding a mandated budget that has to be spent, reported and defended.

Pharma, healthcare and diagnostics

Patient support programmes, access to care models, public-private delivery and therapy-focused design, usually with a disclosure clock or a headquarters mandate running alongside.

HR, safety and operations leaders

Responsible for large outdoor, field or shop-floor workforces where heat exposure has become a duty-of-care question rather than a weather one.

Commercial and marketing leaders

Running a launch, a portfolio decision or an agency roster, and needing someone who has done the job rather than only advised on it.

NGOs, foundations and research organisations

Needing programme design and measurable outcomes to hold funding, or a commercial engine built around work that already matters.

Development finance and UN bodies

Funding adaptation and health resilience, and engaging specialists through advisory roles, empanelment and programme design.

Most arrive with a problem they have not yet named. Naming it properly is where the work begins.

Why the programmes are built this way.

Every programme here runs through the same physical reality: heat that pulls people out of work and out of care, water that cuts the routes medicine travels, air that raises the baseline load on every health system and every employer. Read that first and a programme allocates correctly. Skip it and you get a plan that looked sound in March and stopped working in May.

Climate change is a healthcare story. That is the argument this firm is built on, and it deserves more room than a line on a homepage.
