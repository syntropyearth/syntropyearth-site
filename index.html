What you hold at the end

A season ends. The evidence stays with you.

Three weeks after the last reading you receive one pack. Every item in it is written to be handed to someone: a board, an assurer, a CSR committee, a parent.

  1. The Site Heat Report

    Your season in twelve to fifteen pages: exposure by hour, days in each band, actions taken, cases by grade, outcomes against your own baseline, and three changes for next year.

    Goes toThe board paper and the project file

  2. A heat-safe protocol for your site

    The band card, the rest and water rule, the alert-day actions and the referral path, written for your site and signed by the person who runs it.

    Goes toThe site safety plan

  3. The reporting paragraph

    One paragraph in the disclosure's own voice, with the numbers the season counted.

    Maps toBRSR Principle 3, GRI 403, Schedule VII

  4. Outcomes, counted

    Cases caught early, referrals completed, days lost, attendance on alert days. Counted from the register, never estimated.

    Maps toSDG 3.d and SDG 8.8

Why this, why now

The world has named the risk. India has written the rules. The site is still unmeasured.

A health story first

People act on climate when it is about health.

A 2026 study of more than 30,000 people in Brazil, India, Japan and South Africa found health-framed climate messages twice as likely to shift attitudes as other climate messages. In India, 91 percent of respondents said they were concerned about climate change.

Source: Wellcome Trust and CORE, Climate-health messages build support for climate action, 2026
The rules exist

States now say what a hot day demands.

Karnataka's Heat Action Plan 2026 sets alert bands in wet-bulb globe temperature and gives labour and education officers named duties on alert days. It tells a district what to do. It cannot see inside a private site.

Source: Karnataka Heat Action Plan 2026, KSNDMC
The gap

Classrooms and worksites sit outside the count.

UNICEF counted at least 242 million students whose schooling was disrupted by climate events in 2024, 171 million of them by heatwaves. Public health surveillance counts heat illness at the facility. Nobody counts what happened at the site that morning.

Source: UNICEF, Learning Interrupted, January 2025

That is the piece we build. The health system counts cases at the clinic and trains the system. Syntropy Earth measures exposure and what follows from it at the place of work and learning, paid for by the people who own that place, and hands the result to them.

Where this is going

A climate-health intelligence company, built one measured season at a time.

Every site-season enters one register in one shape: exposure, actions, health, outcomes. One season answers a site's question. Ten seasons give a district a benchmark. Sixty give India something it does not have: a measured picture of what heat does to people at work and at school, site by site.

We are plain about the order. Today the work is measurement and analytics. Forecasting models and machine learning come when the register holds enough seasons to train them on real records, and they will be published with their method. Clinics and anganwadis follow in 2028. A site-level heat-health index follows from 2029.

  • 2027: worksites and schools, first measured seasons
  • 2028: clinics and anganwadis; continuity of care under heat
  • 2029 onward: district benchmarks and the index
