Syntropy Earth / The season

The method · how a measured heat season works

The thermometer is not the measurement. The people are.

A weather feed tells you how hot the city is. A measured season tells you how hot your slab or your classroom was at two in the afternoon, what your site did about it, and what it did to the people there. This page sets out how: the instruments, the bands, the records and the rules we hold ourselves to.

The unit

One site. One season. Everything is counted in site-seasons.

A site is a place with an owner and a roster: a construction site, a plant, a campus, a school. A season is the hot months, 1 March to 15 July in Bengaluru, earlier in Gujarat and Vidarbha, later and more humid in Chennai.

One site measured through one season is a site-season. It is small enough to run well and complete enough to stand by itself. It is also the building block of everything larger: a district benchmark needs several, an index needs many.

The instruments

Why wet-bulb globe temperature, and where it is taken.

Air temperature misses most of what a working body feels. Wet-bulb globe temperature, WBGT, folds four things into one reading: air temperature, humidity, radiant heat from the sun and hot surfaces, and air movement.

Instrument 1

WBGT at the point of work

At the most exposed zone: the slab, the scaffold, the open yard, the playground. On a tripod or bracket at 1.1 metres, away from exhausts and hot walls.

Instrument 2

WBGT at the point of rest

At the rest shed or shaded area. The gap between the two readings shows whether rest on your site is real recovery.

Instrument 3

A shaded reference logger

Air temperature and humidity in the shade, the reading closest to what the weather office reports. It ties your site to the city's record. Schools add loggers in three to six classrooms.

Readings every 15 minutes, synced daily, with a calibration check each fortnight. The method follows IS 16559:2019, the Indian standard for assessing heat stress with WBGT, identical to ISO 7243:2017. Large sites take a third WBGT point.
The bands

A reading becomes an action through four bands.

We did not invent the thresholds. They are the state's. What the season adds is a reading taken at your site, so the right band is on the wall at the right hour.

Greenbelow 26 °C

Normal work. Water at every zone. The card is updated and the hours are counted.

Yellow26 to 28 °C

Water prompted every 20 minutes. A ten-minute shade break each hour for heavy work. The first-aider is briefed.

Orange28 to 31 °C

Heavy outdoor work limited. Fifteen minutes of rest in every hour. Roof and slab work moves to cooler hours. Buddy checks begin.

Redabove 31 °C

Heavy outdoor work stops in the peak hours. The cooling station is staffed. Any case is cooled on site first and transported second.

Bands in wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT), following the Karnataka Heat Action Plan 2026 (KSNDMC). Other states' rules are written into the site's card where they apply.
The four records

Four records a site has never held together.

Most sites have pieces: an attendance sheet, a first-aid book, maybe a thermometer. The season keeps four records against one clock, which is what lets you say that this heat led to that outcome.

The rhythm

Daily, weekly, fortnightly, monthly. Nothing waits for the end.

  • Daily. Readings sync. The band card is updated at 8 am and 1 pm. Supervisors log actions on a phone form.
  • Every Friday. A one-page readout: the week's curve against the bands, hours in each band, actions, cases, one recommendation.
  • Every fortnight. Calibration check. The field doctor visits and reviews cases.
  • Every month. A two-page note to the project director or the CSR committee.
  • Season end. Closing camp, then the evidence pack within three weeks.
Who does what

Your site runs the protocol. We run the measurement.

Your safety officer owns the action protocol inside the site's safety plan. Your supervisors update the card and log what was done. A field doctor runs the screening camps, reviews cases and signs the clinical part of the pack. Syntropy Earth writes the protocol, places the instruments, sends the readouts and writes the evidence pack.

We keep the measuring separate from the delivering on purpose. Camps and distributions are run by the site or by a partner organisation. The people who count the result are not the people being counted.

Data and consent

Codes, never names. Terms agreed before an instrument goes up.

Health information about workers is personal data. Our copy of the register holds site codes and cohorts, never names. Your site keeps its own full record. What enters the shared register is anonymised and enters only on the consent terms you sign on day zero.

No health screening is done on school pupils. Case grades follow the national definitions used by NDMA and the national climate and health programme, so a site's register can be read against what its district reports. Questions of data protection law are settled with counsel in the contract, never assumed on a web page.

Analytics, and what comes after

The analysis is simple by design. The models come when the data can carry them.

In season one the analytics are counts and comparisons anyone can check: hours in each band, actions against bands, cases against exposure, outcomes against your own baseline. A result you can verify with a pencil is worth more to a board than one you have to take on trust.

As site-seasons accumulate, the register supports more: district benchmarks, early-warning thresholds tuned to a kind of work, and forecasting models trained on measured records. We will publish the method for each before it is used on a client's site.

The Heat-Health Site Protocol, the document that fixes all of the above, is in preparation for open publication before the 2027 season, alongside the five methods already published.
